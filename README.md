> **If you are an AI:** read [`AGENTS.md`](AGENTS.md). Everything you need starts there.

Tell an AI what you want built. It uses this repo to design and build it, then
shows you what it made and how it checked it. You decide what happens next.

**You don't need to know how to code, and you don't need to know how this repo works.**

## How to use it

1. **Say what you want.** Plain English. Vague is fine:
   *"Build me an app that tracks when I last watered each of my houseplants."*
2. **Point an AI at this repo.** Clone it next to your project, or give the AI
   the link. Any capable model works: Claude, GPT, Gemini, Copilot, Grok, and
   others, in the terminal, an editor, or the web.
   *"Build the plant-watering app using this repo. Read AGENTS.md first."*
3. **Look at what comes back.** Accept it, reject it, change it, or redirect it.

## What to expect

- **Few questions, often none.** It asks only when your request could honestly
  mean two different things that matter, like who uses it or whether it
  handles money. Say "one shot, don't ask" and it won't: it picks and tells
  you what it guessed. It never asks about names, colors, or tools.
- **It fills the obvious gaps.** Ask for "export" and it considers "import"
  too. It tells you what it added and what it left out on purpose.
- **Nothing goes live without you.** It won't deploy, publish, charge money, or
  message anyone. It hands you the command to do that yourself.
- **An honest handover.** What it guessed, what it built, what it skipped and
  why, and whether the main path actually ran.
- **Want more?** Ask for another round. It fixes what falls short of its own
  promises and lists anything else for you to decide.
